# 숲 입구 수정 시안

Built-in imagegen으로 제작했다. 입력 1은 기존 숲 좌단 시안, 입력 2는 승인된 새벽 숲의 도트 질감·색감 참고다. 큰 가까운 학교 외벽을 제거하고, 나무 사이의 입구 너머에 작은 학교가 멀리 보이도록 수정했다. 생성된 창작 그림은 `distant-school-entrance-v2.png`이며, 게임용 격자와 팔레트로 정리한 결과는 이 폴더의 상위 `Source/`, `Runtime/`, `Preview/`에 있다.

## Prompt

Use case: precise-object-edit. Asset: leftmost viewport of an established 2D belt-scroll pixel-art forest map. Image 1 is the edit target; Image 2 is the definitive art-style and dawn-palette reference. Replace the huge close-up yellow school wall, close door, paved foreground apron and crude small wall in Image 1 with a professionally finished forest ENTRANCE that reveals a SMALL school building FAR AWAY beyond the trees. A two-story 19th-century honey limestone academy with a modest slate roof and arched windows should sit in atmospheric distance in the upper-left background, center roughly one quarter across the canvas, occupying no more than 25% canvas width and 25% canvas height, partially obscured by ordinary leafy trees. A small opening in the forest edge and gently receding narrow lane suggests the entrance toward that remote school; the building itself is tens of meters away. The foreground remains the same broad mostly flat golden sandy belt-scroll walking lane spanning the entire width, with rear edge approximately 47% down, foreground vegetation at bottom 20%, no obstacles across the playable lane. Keep ordinary trees and layered blue-green dawn mist. Right third must remain compatible with the existing forest texture/tree/ground of reference 2 so it can continue horizontally into the unchanged forest. Use the exact rich hand-crafted chunky pixel-art style of Image 2, clean intentional pixel clusters, detailed natural bark/foliage, pale warm yellow sunlight, restrained 19th century gas lamps if any. Maintain landscape ratio around 1360:724. No giant near facade, no giant door, no copied corridor doorway, no cut-and-paste flat rectangles, no paving abruptly pasted on sand, no jungle vines, no characters, no UI, no text, no watermark. The entrance should read as a pleasant gap with a building seen in the distance, not as a wall attached to the forest.
